Essay · Walking

The long walk home

Forty miles of coast path, and the villages that are no longer there. What the map leaves out is the part worth walking for.

ME

By Maya Ellis
6 min read · 12 October 2026

The road ends where the chapel used to be. There is a gate, a stile, and then a mile of gorse that the Ordnance Survey still insists is a lane. I had come for the walk and stayed for the correction: the map is not wrong, exactly. It is simply describing a place that has been gone since 1974.

Everyone I met on the path had a different version of the same story. A school that closed. A ferry that stopped. A postmistress who kept the keys to a building nobody owned. The coast does not erase these things so much as file them under a different heading.

“The road was there. The village was not.”

From chapter two, The Long Walk Home

By the time the light went, I had walked eleven miles and written four pages of notes, three of which were about the weather. The fourth was a list of names, copied from a noticeboard inside the village hall: a committee, it seemed, for a village with no one left to serve on it.
